The leaks we look for on MSP and IT sites

Each one is a decision point a buyer passes before they reach you. The appraisal shows which of them your page settles — and which it leaves open.

i

The homepage that sells to no one in particular

No industry named, no stack named, no buyer named. A visitor cannot tell whether you serve them, so they assume you do not.

ii

"24/7 monitoring" as the whole story

A feature list is not a reason to switch. The appraisal checks whether the page makes the case for moving — or just describes what you run.

iii

The questions a buyer asks before they call

What it costs, who does the work, what happens when something breaks at 2 a.m. Unanswered on the page, they get answered by your competitor.

iv

Proof buried a click deep

Credentials, client tenure and response times found only after the decision — or asked for by a form before a single useful sentence is said.

The questions an IT buyer needs settled before they will talk to you

Your site does not need to answer all of them in full. It needs to not leave a buyer guessing about the ones that decide the call.

Do you cover our stack and our industry?

If the page names no industry, no stack and no integration, the buyer has to assume the answer is no.

What does it cost, and what is included?

No figure and no packaging means the buyer prices you against their own assumption — usually a low one.

Who actually does the work, and where are they?

An IT buyer is buying a person who answers at 2 a.m. If the page does not say who that person is, they cannot buy them.

What happens when something breaks at 2 a.m.?

Response time, escalation path and ownership — the questions that decide which provider gets the call.

Can we talk to a client like us?

Nobody names clients on this site — and your buyers will not trust a wall of logos either. The appraisal checks whether your proof is where your buyer looks.
